Main Freight Routes Between the UK and Saudi Arabia 
There are three primary ways to move cargo between the UK and Saudi Arabia: 
• Air Freight – Best for urgent and high-value shipments.  
• Sea Freight – Most cost-effective for heavy and containerised cargo.  
• Multimodal Transport – Combines road and sea transport for greater flexibility.  
The right option depends on your cargo type, delivery deadline, budget and destination.

Route via the Suez Canal 
The traditional shipping route follows: 
UK → English Channel → Mediterranean Sea → Suez Canal → Red Sea → Jeddah 
Under normal conditions, transit times range from 14 to 20 days, making this the quickest 
sea freight route to western Saudi Arabia. 
Route via the Cape of Good Hope 
Due to ongoing security concerns affecting parts of the Red Sea, some shipping lines 
continue routing vessels around southern Africa. 
The route follows: 
UK → Atlantic Ocean → Cape of Good Hope → Indian Ocean → Arabian Gulf → Dammam 
This alternative route typically takes 28 to 40 days. While the journey is longer, it helps 
carriers maintain operational safety and schedule reliability during periods of regional 
disruption. 
Businesses should confirm routing with their freight forwarder, as shipping schedules 
continue to evolve based on global maritime conditions. 
 
Multimodal Transport 
Multimodal shipping combines multiple transport methods under one logistics solution, 
usually involving road transport within the UK, ocean freight, and final inland delivery in 
Saudi Arabia. 
Typical transit times range from 25 to 30 days, depending on the route and customs 
clearance.

Customs Requirements 
Successful shipping to Saudi Arabia depends on accurate documentation and compliance 
with customs regulations. 
Businesses typically require: 
• Commercial Invoice  
• Packing List  
• Bill of Lading or Air Waybill  
• Certificate of Origin  
• Harmonised System (HS) Codes  
• Import permits (where applicable)  
Saudi Arabia's Zakat, Tax and Customs Authority (ZATCA) oversees customs procedures 
and import compliance. Incorrect documentation or product classification may result in 
shipment delays or additional charges.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs) 
1. What is the fastest freight route between the UK and Saudi Arabia? 
Air freight is the fastest option, with transit times typically ranging from 3–7 days, 
depending on customs clearance and airline schedules. 
2. How long does sea freight take from the UK to Saudi Arabia? 
Sea freight generally takes 14–20 days via the Suez Canal to Jeddah. If vessels are routed 
via the Cape of Good Hope, transit times may extend to 28–40 days. 
3. Which UK ports are commonly used for shipping to Saudi Arabia? 
The main export ports include Felixstowe, Southampton, and London Gateway, offering 
regular container services to Saudi Arabia. 

=== PAGE 9 ===
4. Which Saudi Arabian ports receive cargo from the UK? 
Most shipments arrive through Jeddah Islamic Port, King Abdulaziz Port (Dammam), and 
King Abdullah Port, depending on the cargo's final destination. 
5. What documents are required to ship goods to Saudi Arabia? 
Typical documents include a Commercial Invoice, Packing List, Bill of Lading or Air Waybill, 
Certificate of Origin, HS Codes, and any applicable import permits. 
6. Is sea freight cheaper than air freight? 
Yes. Sea freight is significantly more economical for large, heavy, or containerised 
shipments, while air freight is better suited for urgent and high-value cargo. 
7. What is multimodal freight transport? 
Multimodal freight combines multiple transport methods—such as road and sea—under 
one logistics solution, improving flexibility and efficiency. 
8. Why are some shipments taking longer in 2026? 
Some shipping lines continue to divert vessels around the Cape of Good Hope due to 
security concerns affecting parts of the Red Sea, increasing transit times for certain 
services.
